# Session 01: Learning Paradigms & Problem Framing

**Status:** Completed on 2026-09-05; spaced review required  

## Outcome

Decide whether a request needs ML, identify the learning paradigm from its supervisory signal, and define the unit of prediction, target, horizon, task shape, model metric, business metric, and baseline.

## Study map

- AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI relationships
- Supervised, semi-supervised, self-supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning
- Business request to learning task
- Classification, regression, ranking, clustering, and generation
- Model metric versus business metric and baseline

## Study materials

- [Deep Learning, Chapter 1: Introduction — Goodfellow, Bengio, and Courville](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/contents/intro.html) — Covers AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI relationships.
- [Deep Learning, Chapter 5: Machine Learning Basics — Goodfellow, Bengio, and Courville](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/contents/ml.html) — Covers supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning; classification and regression; performance measures and baselines.
- [Regularization for Deep Learning — Goodfellow, Bengio, and Courville](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/contents/regularization.html) — Covers semi-supervised learning through regularization.
- [A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations — Chen et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/chen20j.html) — Covers self-supervised learning through contrastive representation learning.
- [Introduction to Machine Learning Problem Framing — Google](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/problem-framing) — Covers translating a business request into a learning task, prediction target, metric, and baseline.
