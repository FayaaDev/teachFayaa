F1 — when you need one number

Your tangible win: state the exact situation in which F1 is the right single metric to report.

Worked example: spam filter

A company’s spam filter must block real spam (high recall) while avoiding false positives that send legitimate customer emails to the junk folder (high precision). The two errors cost roughly the same: a missed spam email wastes 30 seconds of employee time; a blocked real email loses a potential sale worth about the same. When the costs are balanced, neither precision nor recall alone tells the full story.

Memory rule: use F1 when the cost of a false positive and a false negative are roughly equal and you must report one number.
