Cross-validation is a rotation

Your tangible win: explain why cross-validation produces several validation scores instead of trusting one arbitrary split.

First, keep a final test set aside

Before choosing a model or tuning settings, set aside a final test set. Do not use it to choose between models. It is the last honest check after decisions are complete.

Cross-validation happens on the remaining training data. It helps you compare choices without committing to one small validation split.

Five-fold cross-validation

Split the training data into five parts, called folds. In round one, train on four folds and validate on the fifth:

Round 1 of 5. In the next round, a different fold becomes validation.

Repeat until each fold has been the validation fold once. This creates five validation scores. Their average is the cross-validation estimate.

Memory rule: k folds → train k models; each model validates on a different fold; average the k scores.

Why rotate?

One validation split can be unusually easy or unusually hard. Rotation checks a model against several held-out portions of the available training data, so a choice is less dependent on one lucky split.

It costs more computation: in five-fold CV, you fit the model five times. That cost is often worthwhile when data is limited or you need to compare model settings fairly.

Where leakage fits

Each round has its own mini train/validation boundary. A scaler, encoder, imputer, or feature selector must fit only on that round’s training folds, then transform its validation fold. A pipeline makes that boundary happen automatically when used with cross-validation.

ActionSafe?Why
Fit a scaler on the four training foldsYesThe validation fold remains unseen while the scaler learns its statistics.
Fit a scaler on all five folds before the roundNoThe validation fold influences the preprocessing step.
