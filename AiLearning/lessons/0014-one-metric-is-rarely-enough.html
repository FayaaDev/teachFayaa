One metric is rarely enough

Your tangible win: name the single business constraint that decides which metric to optimise first.

The production question

Accuracy alone hides the cost of the mistakes that matter. Example: a fraud-detection model trained on 100 000 transactions is 99.2 % accurate. It misses 40 of the 50 actual fraud cases (false negatives). Each missed fraud costs the bank SAR 8 000. The 200 false positives only cost SAR 50 each in manual review. The business therefore cares far more about catching the 40 missed frauds than about the extra 200 reviews.

The right metric is the one that penalises the error the business actually pays for.

Memory rule: start with the cost of a false negative versus a false positive; that ratio names your primary metric.
