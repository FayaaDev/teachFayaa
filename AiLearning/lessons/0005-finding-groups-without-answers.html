Session 03 · 10 minutes · D2

Find groups when nobody supplied the answers

Your tangible win: tell clustering from classification, then make a first safe choice between k-means and DBSCAN.

Classification has answers; clustering looks for them

In classification, the training data already has a label. For example, past transactions say fraud or not fraud. The model learns to predict that known answer.

In clustering, there are no given group labels. You give the model data about customers—such as visits, spending, and products—and ask it to discover groups whose members look similar.

Memory rule: known answer → classification. No answer, find groups → clustering.

k-means: make a chosen number of groups

k-means asks you to choose the number of groups, called k, before it starts. It repeatedly places a center in each group and moves people to the nearest center.

It is a reasonable first candidate when you expect groups that are roughly round and similarly spread out. It is not a good match for long, curved, or unusually shaped groups.

Example: A retailer chooses k = 3 and finds three broad customer groups: low, medium, and high spending. Those names are a human interpretation after clustering—not labels the algorithm received.

DBSCAN: find crowded areas and ignore noise

DBSCAN looks for crowded areas. Points with enough close neighbours form a group. A point far from every crowded area can be marked as noise, meaning it does not belong to a discovered group.

Unlike k-means, DBSCAN does not require you to state the number of groups first. It can also find curved or irregularly shaped groups. However, you must choose what counts as “close” and “crowded,” and it can struggle when groups have very different densities.

“We know fraud or not fraud for old cases.”Classification, not clustering.
“Create 4 broad, round customer segments.”Try k-means; it needs k = 4.

Important: a cluster is a pattern in the data, not automatically a meaningful business segment. Inspect the groups, give them understandable descriptions, and test whether an action based on them is useful.
